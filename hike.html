The 2027 Appalachian Trail Attempt

The Hike

Start in Georgia. Point north. Keep walking.

The route is straightforward. The execution is not. Going the Distance follows a northbound Appalachian Trail thru-hike attempt beginning in early April 2027.

Pledge accounting

What counts as a mile?

A qualifying mile is a unique mile of official Appalachian Trail progress physically completed during the 2027 attempt.

The short version
If it doesn’t advance official Appalachian Trail mileage north, it doesn’t increase the pledge.

Counts

  • Official Appalachian Trail tread physically completed northbound
  • Official trail relocations
  • Required official detours credited as A.T. progress

Does not count

  • Amicalola Approach Trail
  • Side trails and shelter-access trails
  • Town walking
  • Wrong turns and repeated/backtracked miles
  • Shuttle or vehicle travel
  • GPS drift or miscellaneous non-A.T. distance
Whole-mile rule: RallyUp accepts whole-number activity units, so campaign mileage will be rounded down for pledge calculation. Example: 437.8 verified miles becomes 437 pledge miles.

Verification

Progress should be easy to audit.

The campaign is not trying to count every footstep. It is trying to maintain a clear record of official A.T. progress.

  • Official A.T. mileage references
  • Trail/FarOut records and location history
  • Photos, signs, shelters, road crossings, and milestone records
  • Regular RallyUp activity updates
  • Corrections when the source record changes
