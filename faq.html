Frequently Asked Questions

The Useful Details.

The campaign should be easy to explain without requiring anyone to read a terms-of-service document disguised as a story.

For donors

Pledges & donations

What happens if the entire Appalachian Trail is not completed?

Your per-mile pledge is based only on qualifying mileage actually completed. The fundraiser does not require reaching Maine to matter.

Can I pledge after the hike has already started?

Yes. Draft testing confirmed that a late per-mile pledge applies to the total qualifying mileage completed during the campaign, including mileage logged before the pledge was made.

Can I limit how much I might be charged?

Yes. RallyUp supports a donor-set maximum total pledge. The final charge cannot exceed that cap.

When is a per-mile pledge charged?

Per-mile pledges remain pending while the A-Thon is underway and are calculated after the final qualifying mileage is confirmed and the campaign is finalized. One-time donations are processed when made.

What counts as a qualifying mile?

A unique mile of official Appalachian Trail progress physically completed during the 2027 attempt. Side trails, town walking, backtracking, wrong turns, shuttles, and the Amicalola Approach Trail do not increase the pledge total.

How are partial miles handled?

The hiking record can retain fractional mileage, but RallyUp uses whole activity units. The pledge total is rounded down to completed whole miles.

Can I make a normal donation instead?

Yes. The campaign includes a one-time donation path for supporters who prefer not to pledge by the mile.

For businesses

Partnerships

What is the proposed $2,500 Founding Community Partner commitment?

It is designed as two separate commitments: a $2,200 guaranteed charitable commitment intended for the Cancer Research Institute and a $300 Going the Distance campaign support contribution. The two amounts follow separate money paths and are not treated as the same kind of payment.

Is the $2,200 charitable commitment paid to Going the Distance?

No. The intended structure is for the charitable commitment to move through the approved CRI fundraising path. Going the Distance should not receive the $2,200 charitable component. The intent is to use it as a featured matching challenge while ensuring the full $2,200 ultimately benefits CRI, subject to CRI-approved mechanics.

What is the $300 campaign support contribution used for?

It supports the operation, promotion, measurement, maintenance, and long-term documentation of Going the Distance, including partner activation materials, QR and print materials, website and domain costs, software, analytics, outreach, content, reporting, and related campaign administration. It is not represented as a charitable donation to CRI and is not limited solely to one partner's matching window.

Is unused campaign support refunded?

Once a partnership has meaningfully activated, the $300 supports the broader Going the Distance campaign rather than functioning as an expense-by-expense reimbursement account. If the campaign is canceled before meaningful activation or partner benefits begin, treatment of the contribution will be governed by the written partnership terms.

Why split the partnership this way?

The goal is to keep the charitable commitment much larger than the campaign-support component. The $2,200 roughly mirrors one charitable dollar per Appalachian Trail mile, while the $300 helps operate and amplify the campaign so the matching commitment can generate additional public participation.

Does any of this pay for the personal hike?

No. The Appalachian Trail attempt is personally funded. Neither the charitable commitment nor the campaign support contribution is intended to pay for the personal cost of hiking the Trail.

When does a Founding Partner pay?

The partnership should not activate until Going the Distance passes its launch-ready gate and the CRI matching/payment structure is confirmed. A business may express interest or reserve a place before that point.

What if the hike starts but ends early?

The partner is not purchasing a guarantee of Katahdin or a specific Trail milestone. Donor pledges and campaign reporting use actual qualifying mileage completed, while a Featured Matching Partner activation can continue around the actual outcome of the campaign under the written partnership terms.

Does becoming a Going the Distance partner mean CRI endorses the business?

No. A Going the Distance partnership does not imply that CRI sponsors, endorses, or has approved the business unless CRI separately authorizes such a relationship.

Still have a question?
