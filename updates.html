Field Note 001 · Campaign Build · Pre-Launch 2026

Building a 2,200-Mile Fundraiser Before Walking the First Mile

Going the Distance started with a simple idea: instead of making an Appalachian Trail attempt meaningful only if it reaches Katahdin, let the miles themselves carry value.

That changes the architecture of the fundraiser. A donor is not betting on a successful thru-hike. A donor is pledging against verified progress. If the hike ends at 100 miles, the pledge is calculated from 100 miles. If it reaches 1,000, it uses 1,000. If it reaches Maine, every qualifying mile counts.

Maine is the goal. Going the distance is the point.

The practical result is a campaign that can be ambitious without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

Field Note 002 · Methodology · Pre-Launch 2026

What Counts as a Mile?

The cleanest rule turned out to be the simplest one: a pledge mile is a unique mile of official Appalachian Trail progress physically completed during the attempt.

That means side trails, town walking, wrong turns, backtracking, shuttle travel, and the Amicalola Approach Trail do not increase the pledge total. Official relocations and required detours can count when they represent official A.T. progress.

If it doesn’t advance official Appalachian Trail mileage north, it doesn’t increase the pledge.

RallyUp accepts whole-number activity units, so fractional mileage will be rounded down rather than up for pledge calculation.

Field Note 003 · Platform Test · Pre-Launch 2026

The Late-Pledge Question

One of the biggest open questions was whether a donor who joins after the hike begins would pledge only against future miles or against the campaign's total final mileage.

Draft-mode testing answered it: late pledges are retroactive to miles already logged. Maximum caps, penny-per-mile pledges, mileage corrections, one-time gifts, organizer-side pledge edits, privacy behavior, and mobile checkout were also tested successfully.

That makes mid-hike fundraising far more viable. Someone discovering the project at Mile 1,500 can still participate in the entire measured attempt.

Field Note 004 · Partnerships · Pre-Launch 2026

Sponsors Fund the Campaign, Not the Adventure

The Appalachian Trail attempt was planned as a personally funded adventure before a business sponsorship program existed. That should remain true.

Business sponsorship therefore supports the campaign built around the hike: outreach, content, community activation, partner materials, and campaign infrastructure. It is not a request for local businesses to pay for a six-month backpacking trip.

The distinction also reduces risk for early partners. A Founding Community Partner can reserve a position before launch and does not pay until Going the Distance passes a defined launch-ready gate.

Eventually, the field notes will contain an actual field.

For now, the work is building something worth taking to Springer.
