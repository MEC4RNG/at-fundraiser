Business & Community Partnerships

Communities Going the Distance.

A business can help fund the cause and help the campaign reach more people.

The 2027 partner model is built around a simple split: most of the commitment becomes a matching pool for cancer research, while a small campaign-activation contribution pays for the materials and outreach used to put that match to work.

The 2027 model

$2,500 total commitment. Two separate jobs.

The goal is not to ask businesses to spend thousands of dollars merely promoting a fundraiser. The structure puts the great majority of the commitment directly behind the charitable mission while reserving a small, defined amount to activate the campaign around it.

88% / 12%

Most of the commitment goes toward the match.

The $2,200 match is intentionally tied to the scale of the Appalachian Trail—roughly one matching dollar for every mile of the journey. The $300 activation contribution has a narrower purpose: help turn that matching commitment into additional public participation.

$2,200 Matching Pool

Intended to be committed through the approved CRI fundraising path and unlocked by public giving under match terms finalized with CRI.

$300 Campaign Activation

Supports incremental campaign execution such as QR materials, partner-specific creative, local activation, outreach, tracking, and other defined promotion costs.

One Measurable Goal

Use the activation and matching pool to generate more charitable support than the campaign dollars consumed to produce it.

The Appalachian Trail hike remains personally funded. Neither the $2,200 matching pool nor the $300 activation contribution is intended to pay for the personal cost of hiking the Trail.
How the money moves

Two separate paths. One partnership story.

The distinction matters for transparency: a charitable match is not the same thing as a commercial campaign-activation payment.

Charitable match

Business → approved CRI fundraising path
Up to $2,200 reserved for matching public contributions. Going the Distance does not receive this charitable money.

Campaign activation

Business → Going the Distance
$300 supports agreed campaign activation and partner deliverables. It is not represented as a charitable donation to CRI.

What the partner gets

A real activation, not just a logo.

The $300 campaign contribution is expected to have a defined job and measurable output rather than disappearing into a general operating budget.

Visibility

Founding Partner recognition, website placement, partner profile, campaign social recognition, and post-hike acknowledgment.

Local participation

Partner-specific QR materials and match messaging give customers a direct way to pledge, donate, and help unlock the matching pool.

Measurement

Basic referral and activation tracking where practical, with post-campaign reporting on participation and attributable results.

Launch-ready gate

No partner payment while the project is still theoretical.

A business may reserve Founding Partner status before launch. The partnership only activates once Going the Distance can responsibly go public and the CRI match structure is confirmed.

Business terms

Written agreement defines the $2,200 match, $300 activation, deliverables, timing, and what happens if plans change.

If plans change

The partner is not buying a guarantee of Katahdin.

  • If the campaign is canceled before meaningful activation begins, the $300 activation payment is handled according to the written agreement.
  • The $2,200 charitable match remains subject to the approved CRI match terms rather than becoming campaign operating money.
  • If the hike begins but ends early, donor pledges and campaign reporting use actual qualifying mileage completed.
  • If a promised activation deliverable becomes impossible, a comparable benefit or appropriate remedy is agreed with the partner.
Additional ways to help

Cash is not the only useful contribution.

Outdoor brands and A.T.-corridor businesses may also be able to provide donated media, product, printing, lodging, food, digital reach, or other in-kind support that reduces campaign costs or expands the audience.

Where in-kind support replaces a planned cash expense, the campaign can preserve more of its financial resources for direct charitable leverage.

$2,200 to match the miles. $300 to put the match to work.

A deliberately simple partnership designed to make the charitable component larger than the campaign expense.
